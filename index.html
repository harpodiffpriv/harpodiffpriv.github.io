SoK: A Practical Black-Box Framework for Evaluating Differential Privacy in Cyber-Physical Systems

Differential Privacy (DP) techniques have become contenders to improve both the privacy and security of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS). However, comparing research outcomes remains a challenge due to variations in system design and the lack of a comprehensive framework for evaluation. We present Harpo, a black-box scoring framework that quantitatively evaluates research along three dimensions: privacy, utility, and safety.

Introduction

Integrating DP into safety-critical real-time systems poses unique challenges, as privacy mechanisms must not only preserve data but also maintain the system's physical safety and operational utility. This systematization of knowledge (SoK) classifies work into three categories: "Type of Data Protected", "Application Domain", and "Where Noise is Added". Our findings reveal that safety is frequently neglected in privacy-focused CPS literature, highlighting a critical need for the cross-dimensional approach implemented in the Harpo framework.

Harpo: The Black-Box Framework

Harpo functions as a quantitative evaluator. It accepts a paper.json (the artifact being tested) and a weight.json (our standardized criteria) to produce normalized scores [0, 1] for Privacy, Utility, and Safety.

Main Evaluation Function def main(answers_file, weights_file): answers, weights = load(answers_file), load(weights_file) result = {} for cat in ["Privacy", "Utility", "Safety"]: a = answers.get(cat, {}) w = weights.get(cat, {}) result[cat] = round(compute_score(a, w), 3) print(json.dumps(result, indent=2)) Example Output { "Privacy": 0.82, "Utility": 0.76, "Safety": 0.68 }
