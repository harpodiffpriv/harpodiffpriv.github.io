SoK: A Practical Black-Box Framework for Evaluating Differential Privacy in Cyber-Physical Systems

We present a systematization of knowledge (SoK) and a "black-box" scoring framework, Harpo, that quantitatively evaluates papers in this domain along three dimensions: privacy, utility, and safety. We survey and classify the work into three categories ("Type of Data Protected", "Application Domain" and "Where Noise is Added"). We then present insights on how the criteria are obtained and demonstrate how to use our Harpo framework to score papers. Our findings reveal clear patterns, e.g., papers that emphasize privacy mechanisms largely overlap with utility techniques, while safety is neglected in much of the privacy-focused CPS literature. This imbalance highlights the need for a cross-dimensional approach in the design of privacy and safety in future CPS.

Introduction

Privacy has become increasingly important due to the rapid development of autonomous systems (AS) and cyber-physical systems (CPS)[cite: 14]. These systems are used in critical applications such as industrial control and healthcare, raising concerns about the privacy of sensitive data like task execution times and sensor readings[cite: 15, 16].

Differential Privacy (DP) is a promising method for protecting privacy in these systems[cite: 17]. However, integrating DP into safety-critical real-time systems poses challenges, as privacy mechanisms must not only preserve data but also maintain the system's safety and utility [cite: 18-20].

The "Harpo" Black Box Framework

We propose a framework, Harpo, that functions as a black box[cite: 8]. It accepts a research paper (represented as a JSON configuration) as input and produces normalized scores for three key metrics.

As seen in the distribution, few studies achieve high performance in all three indicators simultaneously. Most research optimizes for only two dimensions (e.g., Privacy and Utility), highlighting a gap in the current literature regarding Safety[cite: 393, 394].

Conclusion

We propose a Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) on applying differential privacy (DP) in cyber-physical and autonomous systems, along with Harpo, a practical black-box evaluation framework.

Applying Harpo reveals a consistent pattern: most papers focus on privacy mechanisms and their utility impact but ignore safety as a first-class dimension[cite: 422]. In many studies, "safety" effectively means closed-loop stability under privacy noise, not an explicit physical-risk notion[cite: 424].

This work highlights the need for a cross-dimensional view that explicitly evaluates safety under constraints, adversarial settings, and real-time behavior[cite: 427].
